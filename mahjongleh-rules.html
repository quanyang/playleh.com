MahjongLeh

Rules and Regulations

Updated at 24 August 2026

These rules cover Singapore Classic and the optional house rules offered by MahjongLeh. Singapore Classic rules are always active unless a section is marked optional or availability-gated. Optional rules are disabled by default and must be enabled in Match Setup. Quick Match always uses Singapore Classic; Local Play and custom multiplayer availability are stated below.

General

MahjongLeh closely follows the Singapore Mahjong rules. The game is played with 148 tiles from three different categories — Suits, Honors, and Bonus tiles.

There are 3 types of suit tiles, with 9 numbers per suit and 4 copies of each tile, totaling 108 suit tiles:

  • Circles (筒子)
  • Bamboo (索子)
  • Characters (萬子)

There are 2 types of honor tiles: 4 types of Wind tiles and 3 types of Dragon tiles, with 4 tiles per type, adding up to 28 honor tiles in total

  • Dragon: Red Dragon (紅中), White Dragon (白板), and Green Dragon (發財)
  • Wind: East (东), South (南), West (西), and North (北)

2 types of bonus tiles: 8 types of Flower tiles, 4 types of Animal tiles, adding up to 12 bonus tiles in total:

  • Flower: Spring (春), Summer (夏), Autumn (秋), Winter (冬), Plum (梅), Orchid (蘭), Chrysanthemum (菊), and Bamboo (竹)
  • Animal: Cat (貓), Mouse (老鼠), Rooster (公雞), and Centipede (蜈蚣)

Gameplay

Winning objective

For a standard winning hand, form four sets (melds) and one pair (also called the Eye). A set can be either:

  • Triplet (Pong, 碰): Three identical tiles (e.g., three 5 Circles)
  • Sequence (Chi, 吃): Three consecutive tiles of the same suit (e.g., 3, 4, 5 of Bamboo)
  • Quadruplet (Gang, 槓): Four identical tiles

A pair consists of two identical tiles. Once you have four sets and one pair, you can declare a win (Hu), provided the minimum Tai requirement has been met. More information on Scoring can be found below.

Game Setup

The game is played with 4 players, and the players are seated randomly, with the player seated in the east starting as the dealer. The dealer position rotates to the next player in counterclockwise order, except when the dealer wins, or when the game ends in a stalemate and no Gangs were formed during the game.

Prevailing Wind & Seat Wind

Each round has a Prevailing Wind, and each player is assigned a Seat Wind.

  • Seat Wind: Determined by the player's position at the table (East, South, West, North).
  • Prevailing Wind: Represents the current round wind. The prevailing wind rotates in the order East -> South -> West -> North after each player has served as the dealer.

Game Flow

Each player starts with 13 tiles, and the dealer (East) starts with 14 tiles. Players take turns in counterclockwise order:

  1. Draw: Draw one tile from the wall
  2. Melding using tiles in hand (Optional): After drawing a tile, a player may form a quadruplet meld using tiles in their hand
  3. Discard: Discard one tile face-up
  4. Melding by claiming the discard (Optional): Other players may claim the discarded tile to form a meld

Melding Actions

During gameplay, players can form melds either using the tiles in their hand, or by claiming a discarded tile:

  • Triplet (Pong, 碰): Claim a discarded tile from any player to form a triplet. You must reveal the triplet and discard a tile.
  • Sequence (Chi, 吃): Claim the discarded tile from the player immediately before you to form a sequence. You must reveal the sequence and discard a tile.
  • Quadruplet (Gang, 槓): Form a quadruplet either by:
    • Concealed Gang (暗槓): You have all four identical tiles in hand on your turn.
    • Exposed Gang (明槓): You claim a discarded tile to form a quadruplet with three identical tiles already in your hand.
    • Added Gang (加槓): You add a fourth tile from your hand to an already exposed Pong meld.
    When forming a Gang, you must draw a replacement tile from the dead wall and discard a tile.

Bonus Tiles

When you draw a Flower, or Animal tile, you must:

  1. Reveal the tile immediately
  2. Draw a replacement tile from the dead wall
  3. Continue your turn normally

Bonus tiles do not count toward your hand structure but may provide bonus Tai (台) when calculating your total Tai.

Winning (Hu, 胡)

For a standard winning hand, form four sets and one pair. You can win by:

  • Self-draw (zimo, 自摸): Drawing the winning tile from the wall
  • Win on discard: Claiming another player's discard to complete your hand
  • Robbing the Gang (抢槓): Winning by robbing a tile that another player adds to an already exposed Pong to form an added Gang (加槓). A special exception applies where a player waiting on their final tile to complete Thirteen Terminals (十三幺) may rob a Concealed Gang (暗槓).

Payouts & Scoring

Scoring in MahjongLeh uses a Tai (台)-based scoring system. Winning hands earn Tai based on hand composition and bonuses. The minimum Tai requirement to win is configurable (commonly 1 Tai).

Meld & Tile Bonuses

  • Pong or Gang of Dragons: One Tai for each Dragon Pong or Gang
  • Pong or Gang of Seat Wind: One Tai for a Pong or Gang matching your seat wind
  • Pong or Gang of Prevailing Wind: One Tai for a Pong or Gang matching the prevailing wind
  • Flower matching seat number: One Tai for each Flower matching your Seat Wind's number (East - 1, South - 2, West - 3, North - 4)
  • All four flowers of the same group: Additional one Tai bonus for collecting all four Flower of the same group
  • Animal Tiles: One Tai for each Animal tile
  • All Four Animals: Additional one Tai bonus for collecting all four Animal tiles

Win Bonuses

  • Winning on Last Tile Bonus (海底捞月): Additional one Tai for winning on the last drawable tile in the wall. The bonus does not apply to a replacement tile.
  • Robbing the Gang (抢槓): Additional one Tai bonus for winning by robbing the Gang
  • Replacement Tile Bonus (花上/槓上) - 1 Tai: A self-drawn win from a replacement tile drawn after a Gang or bonus tile
  • Double Gang Bonus (槓槓胡) - 3 Tai: A self-drawn win from the second replacement tile after two consecutive Gangs. It awards 3 Tai instead of the ordinary 1 Tai Replacement Tile Bonus.
  • Concealed Hand (门清) - 1 Tai: One Tai bonus only for a self-drawn win with a fully concealed hand (no exposed melds). Seven Pairs uses its configured final Tai and does not add this bonus.
  • Self-drawn Hand (Zimo, 自摸) - 0 Tai: A normal self-draw adds no Tai. Settlement collects 2u from each of the three opponents.

Singapore Classic Winning Hands — Always Active

These hands and timing awards apply whenever their conditions are met. Compatible awards are added together before the table's maximum Tai cap.

  • Coward Hand (鸡胡) - 0 Tai: A winning hand consisting of any four melds and one pair, provided it does not qualify for any other winning hand combination
  • Ping Hu (平胡) - 4 Tai: A winning hand consisting of no bonus tiles, four Chi melds and one pair. The pair cannot be made up of a dragon tile, prevailing wind tile, or the player's seat wind tile. The hand must also be won by either self-drawn or by waiting for more than one tile option (no Kalong or Dan Diao)
  • All Chow (臭平胡) - 1 Tai: Same rules as Ping Hu, with the exception that the player has bonus tiles
  • All Pong (碰碰胡) - 2 Tai: A winning hand consisting of four Pong or Gang melds and one pair
  • Mixed Suit (半色) - 2 Tai: A winning hand consisting of honor tiles and tiles from only one suit
  • Pure Suit (清一色) - 4 Tai: A winning hand consisting of tiles from only one suit
  • Pure Suit Ping Hu (一色平胡) - 2 Tai bonus: A hand that qualifies for both Ping Hu (4 Tai) and Pure Suit (4 Tai) earns another 2 Tai, totaling 10 Tai before other bonuses.
  • Pure Honors (字一色) - 6 Tai: A winning hand consisting of only honor tiles
  • Three Lesser Scholars (小三元) - 1 Tai: Pongs or Gangs of two Dragons, with the third Dragon as the pair; the other two melds may be any legal melds
  • Three Great Scholars (大三元) - 10 Tai: A Pong or Gang of each of the three Dragons; the remaining meld and pair may be any legal tiles
  • Four Lesser Blessings (小四喜) - 2 Tai: Pongs or Gangs of three Winds, with the fourth Wind as the pair; the remaining meld may be any legal meld
  • Four Great Blessings (大四喜) - 13 Tai: A Pong or Gang of each of the four Winds, plus one pair
  • Mixed Terminals (混老头) - 2 Tai: A winning hand consisting of only terminal tiles (1s and 9s) and honor tiles
  • Pure Terminals (清老頭) - 13 Tai: A winning hand consisting of only terminal tiles (1s and 9s) and no honor tiles
  • Eighteen Arahats (十八罗汉) - 13 Tai: A winning hand consisting of four Gang melds and one pair
  • Thirteen Terminals (十三幺) - 13 Tai: A winning hand consisting of one of each terminal suit tile (1 and 9) and each honor tile, plus a duplicate tile to form the pair
  • Heavenly Hand (天胡) - 10 Tai: The dealer begins with a complete winning hand before any discard. Opening Flower and Animal replacements remain eligible.
  • Earthly Hand (地胡) - 8 Tai: A non-dealer completes a winning hand on their first self-draw, before anyone at the table has made a real Chow, Pong, or Gang. Singleton Flower or Animal reveals do not disqualify it.
  • Nine Heavenly Gates (九连宝灯) - 6 Tai: A fully concealed 1112345678999 hand in one numbered suit, plus any duplicate rank from that suit. Any exposed meld or declared concealed Gang disqualifies it. It also receives Pure Suit.
  • Hidden Treasure (Four Concealed Pongs, 四暗刻) - 6 Tai: Four concealed Pong or Gang sets plus a pair. It may be self-drawn, or won from a discard only when that tile completes the pair, not the fourth Pong, and never by Robbing the Gang. It also receives All Pong; a self-drawn win also receives the ordinary Concealed Hand bonus.

Seven Pairs (七对子) — Optional House Rule

Seven Pairs is disabled by default and is not part of Singapore Classic. A player must enable and configure it for Local Play, or a host must do so for a supported custom multiplayer lobby. Quick Match remains Singapore Classic.

Eligibility
  • The winning shape contains exactly 14 non-bonus tiles counted as exactly seven pairs.
  • No Chow, Pong, or Gang may have been declared.
  • Flowers and Animals remain separately revealed bonus tiles outside the 14-tile shape.
  • Every non-bonus tile appears exactly two or four times. Two identical tiles count as one pair.
  • Four identical undeclared tiles follow the configured setting. If they count as one pair, the quad cannot be part of a valid 14-tile Seven Pairs hand; if they count as two pairs, the quad contributes two pairs.
  • The winning source must be allowed by the settings. Discard wins still respect Sacred Discard and Missed Discard; other normal legality gates apply where relevant.

Scoring
  • By default, a self-drawn win receives Seven Pairs 3 Tai + Self-drawn Hand 0 Tai = 3 Tai before other eligible awards. The zero-Tai row makes clear that no hidden Tai was added.
  • When the configured winning-source setting permits those sources, a discard or robbed-added-Gang win uses the Seven Pairs 2 Tai discard award. A robbed added Gang may still receive the separate Robbing the Gang award.
  • Self-draw still uses the normal payment calculation: each opponent pays the self-draw amount. Only the Seven Pairs Tai award changed; settlement was not replaced by another bonus.
  • Seven Pairs does not also receive a Ping Hu, All Chow, All Pong, or Pure Suit Ping Hu award, even when the tiles could form a standard hand.
  • When suit and honor stacking is enabled, at most one compatible color-family award applies: Mixed Suit (Half Suit), Pure Suit, or Pure Honors.
  • Four identical tiles used as pairs trigger neither a Gang payout nor a Dragon or Wind triplet bonus.
  • Compatible event and bonus awards, the minimum and maximum Tai settings, Shooter mode, credit value, and the normal payment calculation still apply.
Multiplayer Availability

Every seated human in a custom lobby needs a compatible app version. An unsupported member cannot enable or start Seven Pairs, or join or rejoin a lobby while Seven Pairs is active. Quick Match remains Singapore Classic.

Optional House Rules — Local Play Only

These Local Play house rules are disabled by default and are not available in online tables.

  • Humanly Hand (人胡) - 5 Tai: A non-dealer wins on an ordinary discard before their own first draw and before anyone at the table has made a real Chow, Pong, or Gang. Singleton Flower or Animal reveals do not disqualify it. It does not apply when Robbing the Gang.
  • Pure Green Suit (绿一色) - 2 Tai: Every non-bonus tile is Green Dragon (發) or 2, 3, 4, 6, or 8 Bamboo. It adds to a valid standard hand, and to Seven Pairs only when suit and honor stacking is enabled.

Honor Instant Wins — Optional House Rule

This house rule is disabled by default and is available in Local Play and custom multiplayer; Quick Match remains Singapore Classic. Three Great Scholars (大三元) and Four Great Blessings (大四喜) can be enabled independently. The ordinary complete-hand versions listed above remain part of Singapore Classic; these toggles additionally end the hand as soon as the enabled third distinct Dragon set or fourth distinct Wind set is completed, without requiring four sets and a pair. The win receives the hand's listed Tai plus eligible Flower, Animal, Dragon, and Wind bonuses. The configured minimum Tai still applies.

  • Completion in the initial hand, by self-draw, or by a concealed Gang uses self-draw settlement.
  • Completion by claiming a discard for Pong or Gang uses discard-win settlement, including the Shooter rule. An added Gang does not trigger an Honor Instant Win.

Automatic Flower Wins — Not House-Rule Toggles

These terminal wins apply automatically in Local Play and have no Match Setup toggle. Online, they apply only when every seated player supports action:robbing_the_eighth:v1 and the online feature is enabled; that unanimous capability snapshot is frozen when the game starts. Unsupported online tables continue classic Flower reveal and replacement play. Only the eight distinct Flower tiles count; Animals do not. If the drawer owns all eight Flowers, that win takes priority. Otherwise, a player already holding the other seven may rob the newly revealed eighth Flower.

  • All Flowers and Seasons (八仙过海) - 10 Tai: The drawer reveals their eighth distinct Flower and wins immediately by self-draw.
  • Robbing the Eighth (七抢一) - 6 Tai: A player holding seven distinct Flowers claims the eighth from its drawer and wins immediately. Passing leaves the Flower with the drawer and replacement play resumes.

Both Flower terminal wins bypass the configured minimum Tai. Their winning-hand Tai and eligible Flower and Animal awards are combined, then the table's maximum Tai cap is applied once for payout. With u = credits per Tai × 2(awarded Tai − 1), All Flowers pays the winner 6u, with each opponent paying 2u. For Robbing the Eighth, the drawer is the Shooter: with Shooter enabled, the drawer pays 4u and the other players pay nothing; without Shooter, the drawer pays 2u and each bystander pays 1u. The winner receives 4u in either case.

Payment Variants — Optional House Rules

Both Payment switches are independent and disabled by default. Local Play always offers them; a custom-lobby host may use them when compatible multiplayer support is available. Quick Match uses Singapore Classic payments.

  • Gang Shang: Discarder Pays All: Only an Exposed (Visible) Gang claimed directly from another player's discard starts responsibility. If the Gang player self-draws during the same uninterrupted winning turn, the original discarder pays the full ordinary self-draw total and the other two opponents pay nothing. The immediate Gang declaration payment still applies. If a Flower or Animal appears during the replacement sequence, the final non-bonus tile becomes the latest replacement tile. A later Concealed or Added Gang continues responsibility only when that latest replacement tile completes it. Declaring a Gang that was already complete before the replacement ends responsibility. Responsibility also ends when the Gang player discards, the turn or player changes, a replacement draw fails, or a Gang is cancelled or robbed. Passing a robbery window keeps active responsibility. A Concealed or Added Gang does not create responsibility on its own.
  • Refund Gang Payment When Robbed: Gang payment remains immediate, as in Singapore Classic. After a confirmed Robbing the Gang win becomes public, the exact payment for that Gang is reversed once before the usual Hu settlement. Passing, an invalid or cancelled robbery, or a Gang that is not robbed causes no refund. The refund does not change the Hu settlement or reveal whether another player could rob the Gang; it also covers the rare permitted robbery of a Concealed Gang for Thirteen Terminals.
  • When both are enabled: A robbed Gang is paid, refunded once, then settled as an ordinary Robbing the Gang win. No Gang Shang responsibility remains. If the Gang is not robbed and the uninterrupted chain ends in self-draw, its immediate payment remains and the original discarder covers the self-draw settlement.

Instant Payout

Certain actions trigger immediate credit exchanges before the hand ends. Instant payouts are settled immediately and are not affected by the final outcome of the hand. Instant payouts are disabled once the last drawable tile has been drawn.

  1. Exposed Gang (明槓) - 20 credits: Each player will pay 20 credits to the player who formed the Gang.
  2. Concealed Gang (暗槓) - 40 credits: Each player will pay 40 credits to the player who formed the Gang.
  3. Pair of Flower tiles matching Player's seat (咬到) - 20 credits: If a player draws both Flower tiles that matches their seat number, every other player will pay 20 credits to the player.
  4. Matching Animal tiles (咬到) - 20 credits: If a player draws a matching pair of Animal tiles (e.g. Rooster and Centipede, or Cat and Mouse), every other player will pay 20 credits to the player.
  5. Bitten (咬到) before replacement tiles - 40 credits: For the above mentioned 咬到 events (3 and 4), if they were done before any replacement tiles were drawn, the player receives 40 credits from each player instead of 20.
  6. All four flowers of the same group - 40 credits: If a player draws all four Flower tiles of the same group, every other player will pay 40 credits to the player.
  7. All four Animal tiles - 40 credits: If a player draws all four Animal tiles, every other player will pay 40 credits to the player.

Shooter Rule

When enabled, the Shooter rule applies additional penalties to the Shooter who discards a tile that leads to an instant payout or a win. The shooter is responsible for paying on behalf of everyone.

The shooter rule only applies in the following scenarios:

  • When a player wins by claiming a discarded tile, the player who discarded the winning tile is known as the Shooter.
  • When a player forms an Exposed Gang meld by claiming a discarded tile, the player who discarded the tile is known as the Shooter.
  • When a player wins by Robbing the Gang, the player who formed the Gang is known as the Shooter.

Ending the Game

Dead Wall

The dead wall is a reserve of 15 tiles used for: Bonus tile draws, Gang replacement draws. Tiles in the dead wall are not part of the drawable wall.

Game End Conditions

A game ends when:

  • A player wins
  • The game ends in a stalemate when there are no more drawable tiles remaining in the live wall (excluding the 15 tiles in the dead wall). When a player draws the last drawable tile, they need not discard any tile (you might be asked to select one, but it is not actually discarded). After drawing the last tile, the player will not be allowed to draw anymore replacement tiles for bonus tiles or Gang, and will not get any instant payouts as well. The player is allowed to win using the last drawn tile.

If the game ends without a winner, it is considered a stalemate, and no points are exchanged.

Special discard Rules

Sacred & Missed Discard Rules

MahjongLeh enforces the Sacred Discard (回头牌) and Missed Discard (过水牌) rule to prevent players from claiming tiles they have previously declined.

  • Sacred Discard: If a player discards a tile, they may not later claim the same tile from another player's discard for a Win, Pong, or Chow.
    • The restriction on the sacred tile is lifted after the player has discarded a different tile.
  • Missed Discard: If a player passes on a discarded tile and did not claim it, they may not later claim the same tile from another player's discard for a Win, or Pong.
    This rule does not apply if a player missed the opportunity because another player's action took precedence (e.g. player A wanted to Chow a tile, but player B Pong took precedence instead, in this case the tile is not considered a Missed Discard for player A).
    • The restriction on the missed tiles are lifted after the player has drawn a tile or claimed another player's discarded tile.

Swap Calling (换吃) Rule

MahjongLeh enforces the Swap Calling (换吃) rule.

  • When a player Chows (吃) a discarded tile to form a sequence, they are not allowed to immediately discard:
    • The same tile that was used to Chow, or
    • A tile on the opposite end of the same sequence
  • Example: If a player Chows a 4 to form 4-5-6, they may not discard a 4 or 6 in that turn.
  • This rule prevents players from effectively swapping tiles through repeated Chow actions.
  • The restriction applies only to the discard made immediately after the Chow.

Glossary

  • Kalong (卡张): Waiting on the middle tile of a sequence (e.g., waiting for 5 to complete 4-5-6).
  • Dan Diao (单吊): Waiting on a single tile to complete the final pair.
